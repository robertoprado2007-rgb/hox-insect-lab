About and Sources

What this site is

Hox Insect Lab is a free website for kids and their teachers. It shows how a small group of genes, called Hox genes, helps decide what an insect's body parts look like. You can read short lessons, then switch genes on and off in a 3D insect to see what the model predicts.

The site has a Learn page, a Lab, a glossary and a page for teachers.

How it works

  • Real gene data. The gene records come from a public science database.
  • Rules from real experiments. The rules are based on experiments that scientists published. We list them below.
  • A 3D model. The Lab turns the rules into a 3D insect that you can turn around and zoom.
  • Honest labels. Each result says if it was seen in a real experiment or if it is the model's guess.

An honest note. The 3D insects are predictions from simple rules. They are not photos of real animals. Real animals are more complicated.

Where the data comes from

Gene records come from NCBI Datasets, a public database. The site uses records for the eight Hox genes in 327 insect species. These species belong to 15 insect orders. Some species have no record for some genes. That only means the database has no entry, not that the animal lacks the gene.

The rules use 44 published experiment records. They come from 34 scientific papers. Open the list to see them.
